2. SLASHING — REAL GEOMETRY, NOT PARTICLE-ONLY
Every visible world surface must respond to slash attempts. A practical implementation may classify objects as (A) physically separable mesh, (B) pre-fractured architectural assembly, (C) large world/terrain proxy that produces a persistent cut/decal and localized detachable chunks. This keeps the promise of cutting any target without demanding an impossible unbounded global mesh split on mobile. UI must distinguish real detach from cosmetic terrain marks.
For supported dynamic meshes: determine swept slash plane in world coordinates, find intersecting triangles, clip polygons to both sides, weld edge vertices, generate sealed interior cap surfaces with cut material/UVs, compute mass and inertia, replace original with two collision bodies, impart directional impulse and torque, and preserve ownership of fragments. Avoid splitting near-zero fragments; cap fragment counts; pool fragments and despawn safely after persistence window. For building sections: structural connectivity graph, sever supports, collapse detached components, fracture masonry, dust clouds, collision sound, nearby debris reactions. Large static architecture: pre-fractured panels and detachable roof/bridge modules plus persistent cuts.
Slash sequence: 90-150 ms anticipation -> 100-180 ms rapid strike -> 120-220 ms recovery. On solid hit: 35-70 ms hit-stop (do not pause critical physics indefinitely), directional camera impulse, controlled camera shake 0.15-0.35 s, sword hand recoil, bass impact sound, sharp cut accent, 3D debris and dust, impact flash under accessibility limit. Shake scales with mass and impact, never nonstop; allow strength slider and off switch. Make a missed slash visibly and audibly different from a hit. Validate collisions at high velocity using sweep tests.
Acceptance tests: slash an isolated wall at three angles; wall visibly divides and fragments move; slash a roof and detach a piece; slash a tower support and trigger bounded collapse; slash an enemy and trigger hit reaction; slash an unbreakable terrain region and show a persistent cut mark; test 20 slashes without memory runaway; verify cut works at 30 and 60 fps.

3. BRUSH-HOOK — MANDATORY MOVEMENT MECHANIC
Touch-and-hold brush icon enters targeting; camera raycast checks surfaces and paints an ink glyph on a valid wall or rooftop within range. A valid anchor must show a clear outline, distance and feedback. On release, fire a visible ink tether from hand/brush to glyph. Support two distinct actions: TAP to pull toward anchor; HOLD to swing on a tether. Maintain rope length and tension constraint, damping, momentum conservation, velocity steering, anchor reeling, and safe release. Allow chaining anchors midair, reattachment cooldown and cancel button. Wall-run can start when tether brings player close to a suitable wall; corner handling and fall recovery required. Invalid surface must give a clear red/grey glyph and no hook.
Suggested controls: left thumb joystick move; right drag look; jump tap; jump double tap while airborne for air dash if unlocked; sword icon tap quick slash, hold charge heavy slash; brush-hook icon tap aim/fire to surface, hold to swing, release to detach (choose a consistent implementation and show it in tutorial); paint mode toggle to place runes; slide/crouch button; pause upper-right. Never overlap control zones; allow size/position/opacity adjustments.
Acceptance tests: paint anchor on side wall, tether appears, pull player from rooftop to wall; swing across a street with visible pendulum; detach and land; chain two hooks; try an invalid surface and get feedback; retain camera control while swinging; run at 30 fps on mid-range Android.
